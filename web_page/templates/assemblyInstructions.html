Introduction & Set Up

Welcome

The SonicSole integrates pressure sensors, an IMU (inertial measurement unit), and a microcontroller, enabling various activities. Its modular design allows for quick setup and efficient data collection. In industry applications, data from pressure sensors and IMUs can be used for gait analysis, balance assessment, rehabilitation monitoring, and performance optimization in sports and healthcare settings.

Assembly Instructions

1️⃣ Collect the hardware assembly, 3d printed case, logic unit, and 2 foam sheets.
2️⃣ Select a set of 2 insoles based on your shoe size.
3️⃣ Put the logic unit in the 3d printed case and close it.
    a) Orient the logic unit so that the battery is at the bottom of the case and the cable connector is on top.
    b) Make sure the speaker fits into the designated indent at the bottom of the case.
4️⃣ Put both insoles face down on the foam sheet and use a marker/writing utensil to outline the insoles.
5️⃣ Cut the foam sheet along your outlines.
6️⃣ Tape the hardware assembly onto the insole which corresponds to your dominant foot.
    a) Remove the backing paper on both pressure sensors and stick them onto the indicated positions (based on the diagram).
    b) Fold the large white wire along the insole and tape it down so that the end of the wire comes out of the insole near the heel.
    c) Use tape to secure any loose wires and hardware components.
7️⃣ Glue the foam on top of both your insoles with tacky glue and wait 5-10 minutes until it dries.
    a) Only apply glue onto the edges of the insole with the hardware assembly inside to not disturb the wiring.
8️⃣ Plug the large white wire coming out of the insole with the hardware into the logic unit through the slot in the 3d printed case.
9️⃣ Place both insoles inside your shoes.
🔟 Clip the 3d printed case securely to your shoe.

Hardware Assembly

The hardware assembly consists of two Force Sensing Resistors (FSRs) mounted under the heel and forefoot. These sensors vary their resistance inversely with applied pressure, producing analog voltage signals proportional to foot force.

The FSRs connect to an Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) interface that digitizes the signals for processing. The system uses the Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI) bus to communicate with ADCs that read heel and forefoot pressure sensor data, performing synchronous serial data transfers.

The hardware assembly also contains an IMU (inertial measurement unit). An IMU is used to measure position. Specifically, the SonicSole takes advantage of the IMU's accelerometer. The system uses UART (Universal Asynchronous Receiver/Transmitter) communication to receive continuous data from the IMU, transmitting readings to the Raspberry Pi through a serial interface.

The hardware assembly is embedded within the insole, which was designed in Solidworks (a CAD (computer-aided design) software). and 3D printed using a flexible filament, TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane).

Logic Unit

The logic unit, housed in a 3D printed case, is the control center of the system. It contains a Raspberry Pi and a HAT (hardware attached on top) accessory board for interfacing with the sensors.

The Pi runs a script that continuously reads values from the sensors and packages the data into UDP (User Datagram Protocol) packets for transmission.

Power is supplied by a rechargeable 3.7V LiPo (Lithium Polymer) battery. The device also includes an audio amplifier and miniature speaker.

Data Communication (UDP)

The system transmits sensor data using UDP (User Datagram Protocol), a connectionless communication method ideal for real-time applications.

Each packet contains the sensor readings encoded as bytes and sent over the local network to the host (also the Raspberry Pi) running the Flask web interface.

On the receiving end, the Flask server decodes these packets, uses the data for various applications.
